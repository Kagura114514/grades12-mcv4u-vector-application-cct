Chapter 7 · Lesson 7.6

Cross Product
of Two Vectors

Calculate a vector perpendicular to two 3D vectors, and interpret its direction and magnitude.

3D vectorsRight-hand rulePerpendicular vector

IN THIS LESSON

Key ideasComponent formulaWorked examplePractice prompt

01 · Key ideas

The cross product returns a vector.

For vectors a and b in three-dimensional space, a × b is perpendicular to both. Its direction is determined by the right-hand rule. Reversing the order changes the direction: b × a = −(a × b).

The magnitude depends on the sine of the included angle. The cross product is zero if either vector is the zero vector, or if both vectors are nonzero and parallel or antiparallel. For nonzero vectors, these are exactly the cases when their cross product is zero.

|a × b| = |a||b| sin θ

02 · Component formula

Compute each coordinate carefully.

a × b = (a₂b₃ − a₃b₂, a₃b₁ − a₁b₃, a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)

After calculating, verify perpendicularity by checking (a × b) · a = 0 and (a × b) · b = 0.

03 · Original worked example

Find a perpendicular vector

Let a = (1, 2, 0) and b = (0, 1, 3). Find a × b.

1First coordinate: a₂b₃ − a₃b₂ = 2(3) − 0(1) = 6.
2Second coordinate: a₃b₁ − a₁b₃ = 0(0) − 1(3) = −3.
3Third coordinate: a₁b₂ − a₂b₁ = 1(1) − 2(0) = 1.
4Therefore a × b = (6, −3, 1). Its dot product with each original vector is zero.

04 · Practice prompt

Check your understanding

Find i × j using the right-hand rule. Then explain why j × i points in the opposite direction.

Show a hint

The standard unit vectors i, j, k form a right-handed coordinate system.
