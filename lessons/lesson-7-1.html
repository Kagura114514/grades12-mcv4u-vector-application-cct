Chapter 7 · Lesson 7.1

Vectors as Forces

Represent forces with vectors, combine them to find a resultant, and determine what force would balance a system.

ResultantEquilibrantEquilibrium

IN THIS LESSON

Key ideasMethodWorked examplePractice prompt

01 · Key ideas

Forces have magnitude and direction.

A force can be represented by a vector. When several forces act on an object, their vector sum is the resultant force. If the resultant is zero, the forces are in equilibrium.

The equilibrant is the force that would balance a given resultant. It has equal magnitude and opposite direction.

R = F₁ + F₂ + ··· + Fₙ
E = −R

For forces that do not lie along the same line, resolve each force into components along perpendicular axes. Add the horizontal components together and the vertical components together.

02 · A reliable method

Choose axes, resolve, then add.

  1. Draw and label each force vector.
  2. Choose convenient perpendicular axes and a positive direction.
  3. Resolve angled forces into components.
  4. Add the components to get the resultant vector.
  5. Use the magnitude and direction of the resultant to describe the equilibrant.

03 · Original worked example

Two perpendicular forces

A 3 N force acts east and a 4 N force acts north. Find the resultant and equilibrant.

1Choose east as +x and north as +y. The force vectors are (3, 0) N and (0, 4) N.
2Add components: R = (3, 0) + (0, 4) = (3, 4) N.
3Magnitude: |R| = √(3² + 4²) = 5 N. Direction: tan θ = 4/3, so θ ≈ 53.1° north of east.
4The equilibrant is E = (−3, −4) N: 5 N, 53.1° south of west.

04 · Practice prompt

Check your understanding

Two forces of 6 N east and 8 N south act on an object. Set up the resultant in component form, then determine its magnitude and the equilibrant.

Show a hint

Use east as positive x and north as positive y. The southward component is negative.
