Chapter 7 · Lesson 7.3

Dot Product:
Geometric Vectors

Use vector magnitudes and the included angle to calculate a scalar product and interpret its sign.

MagnitudeIncluded angleScalar

IN THIS LESSON

DefinitionInterpreting the signWorked examplePractice prompt

01 · Definition

The dot product returns a scalar.

Place two geometric vectors tail to tail and let θ be their included angle from 0° to 180°. Multiply their magnitudes by the cosine of that angle.

a · b = |a||b| cos θ

This definition works whether the vectors are drawn in a plane or in three-dimensional space. Unlike the original vectors, the result is a single number.

02 · Interpreting the sign

The angle tells you the sign.

Acute angle

cos θ > 0, so a · b is positive.

Right angle

cos θ = 0, so a · b = 0.

Obtuse angle

cos θ < 0, so a · b is negative.

If both vectors are nonzero, a zero dot product means they are perpendicular.

03 · Original worked example

Use magnitudes and angle

Two vectors have magnitudes 6 and 5, with an included angle of 60°. Find their dot product.

1Start with a · b = |a||b| cos θ.
2Substitute: a · b = 6(5)cos 60°.
3Since cos 60° = 1/2, the dot product is 15.

04 · Practice prompt

Check your understanding

Vectors p and q have magnitudes 4 and 7, and their included angle is 120°. Is p · q positive or negative? Find its exact value.

Show a hint

Use cos 120° = −1/2. An obtuse angle produces a negative dot product.
