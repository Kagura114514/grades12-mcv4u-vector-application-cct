Chapter 7 · Lesson 7.7

Applications of
Dot & Cross Products

Connect vector products to physical quantities: work, area, and torque.

WorkAreaTorque

IN THIS LESSON

WorkAreaTorquePractice prompt

01 · Dot product application

Work measures force along displacement.

Only the component of a force in the direction of displacement contributes to the work done by that force. The dot product automatically selects this component.

W = F · d = |F||d| cos θ

Work is measured in joules (J), equivalent to newton-metres. A force perpendicular to the displacement does no work in this model.

02 · Cross product application

Cross product magnitude gives area.

Parallelogram area = |a × b|
Triangle area = ½|a × b|

The cross product vector is perpendicular to the plane of the two side vectors; its magnitude is the parallelogram area they span.

03 · Rotational effect

Torque depends on lever arm and angle.

The torque vector describes the turning effect of a force about a point. Let r point from the pivot to the point where the force is applied. The direction of r × F gives the rotation axis.

τ = r × F
|τ| = |r||F| sin θ

Torque magnitude is greatest when the force is perpendicular to the lever arm and when the force is applied farther from the pivot.

04 · Original worked example

Work by a pulling force

A 10 N force pulls an object 5 m along a horizontal floor. The force is directed 60° above the direction of travel. Find the work done by this force.

1Use W = |F||d|cos θ.
2Substitute: W = 10(5)cos 60°.
3W = 50(0.5) = 25 J.
4The upward part of the force does not contribute to the horizontal displacement.

05 · Practice prompt

Choose the right product

For each quantity, decide whether a dot product or cross product is the natural model: (a) work by a force, (b) area of a triangle formed by two side vectors, (c) turning effect of a force about a hinge.

Show a hint

Work uses the component along displacement. Area and torque depend on the perpendicular component.
