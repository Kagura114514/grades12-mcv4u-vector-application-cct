Chapter 7 · Lesson 7.2

Velocity

Combine velocity vectors to model motion relative to the ground, including boats in currents and aircraft in wind.

Relative velocityNavigationVector addition

IN THIS LESSON

Key ideasNavigation methodWorked examplePractice prompt

01 · Key ideas

Motion depends on the reference frame.

A boat's velocity through still water and the current's velocity relative to shore combine to give the boat's velocity relative to shore. The same model applies to an aircraft moving through air while wind moves relative to the ground.

vground = vcraft/air + vcurrent/wind

Pay attention to direction words such as “from the east”: a wind from the east travels toward the west. Keep units consistent before adding velocity vectors.

02 · Navigation method

Model each motion separately.

  1. Choose axes and state the positive directions.
  2. Write the vehicle's velocity relative to the water or air.
  3. Write the current or wind velocity relative to the ground.
  4. Add the vectors to obtain ground velocity.
  5. Use components to find ground speed, direction, time, or drift.

03 · Original worked example

Aircraft with a crosswind

An aircraft travels north at 240 km/h relative to the air. Wind blows east at 50 km/h. Find its ground speed and direction.

1Let east be +x and north be +y. Then vaircraft/air = (0, 240) and vwind = (50, 0), in km/h.
2Ground velocity: vground = (0, 240) + (50, 0) = (50, 240) km/h.
3Ground speed = √(50² + 240²) ≈ 245.2 km/h.
4Angle east of north: θ = tan⁻¹(50/240) ≈ 11.8°.

04 · Practice prompt

Check your understanding

A boat points due north at 4 m/s relative to still water. A river current flows east at 3 m/s. Find the boat's velocity relative to shore.

Show a hint

Represent north as +y and east as +x. Add the two perpendicular component vectors.
