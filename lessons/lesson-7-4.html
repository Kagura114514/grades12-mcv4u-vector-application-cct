Chapter 7 · Lesson 7.4

Dot Product:
Algebraic Vectors

Calculate the dot product from components, test perpendicularity, and find angles between vectors.

ComponentsPerpendicularityAngle

IN THIS LESSON

Component formulaUseful testsWorked examplePractice prompt

01 · Component formula

Multiply corresponding components.

For algebraic vectors, the geometric dot product can be evaluated directly by multiplying corresponding components and adding the results.

(a₁, a₂, a₃) · (b₁, b₂, b₃)
= a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃

In two dimensions, omit the third component: (a₁, a₂) · (b₁, b₂) = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂.

02 · Useful tests

One calculation answers several questions.

  • Perpendicular? For nonzero vectors, a · b = 0 exactly when they are perpendicular.
  • Angle? cos θ = (a · b) / (|a||b|).
  • Magnitude? a · a = |a|².

When finding an angle, first calculate each vector's magnitude and check that neither vector is zero.

03 · Original worked example

Calculate and test

Let a = (2, −1, 3) and b = (4, 2, 0). Calculate a · b and decide whether the vectors are perpendicular.

1Multiply corresponding components: 2(4) + (−1)(2) + 3(0).
2Add: a · b = 8 − 2 + 0 = 6.
3Because the dot product is not zero, these nonzero vectors are not perpendicular.

04 · Practice prompt

Check your understanding

Let u = (1, 2, −1) and v = (3, 0, 3). Calculate u · v. What does your result tell you about the angle between them?

Show a hint

Multiply the x, y, and z components in matching positions and add.
