7.1

Vectors as Forces

A force has magnitude and direction, so it can be represented by a vector. Add force vectors component by component to find the resultant. The equilibrant has the same magnitude as the resultant but points in the opposite direction. Equilibrium occurs when the vector sum of all forces is zero.

R = F₁ + F₂ + ···   |   E = −R

Example. A 3 N eastward force and a 4 N northward force act on an object. In east-north components, R = (3, 4) N, so |R| = √(3² + 4²) = 5 N. Its direction is about 53.1° north of east. The equilibrant is 5 N in the opposite direction.

Try next: Draw a free-body diagram, choose perpendicular axes, and resolve angled forces into components before summing.

7.2

Velocity

Velocity is a vector. In relative-motion problems, the observed or ground velocity is the vector sum of the object's velocity through its medium and the medium's velocity relative to the ground. For navigation, distinguish the direction the boat or aircraft points from the direction it actually travels.

vground = vcraft/air + vcurrent/wind

Example. An aircraft moves north at 240 km/h through still air while wind moves it east at 50 km/h. Its ground velocity is (50, 240) km/h. The ground speed is √(50² + 240²) ≈ 245.2 km/h, at about 11.8° east of north.

Check: State the reference frame for every velocity and convert units before combining quantities.

7.3

Dot Product: Geometric Vectors

The geometric dot product combines the magnitudes of two vectors with the cosine of their included angle. Its result is a scalar. An acute angle gives a positive result, a right angle gives zero, and an obtuse angle gives a negative result.

a · b = |a||b| cos θ   (0° ≤ θ ≤ 180°)

Example. If |a| = 6, |b| = 5, and θ = 60°, then a · b = 6(5)cos 60° = 15. If both magnitudes are nonzero, a · b = 0 means the vectors are perpendicular.

Use it to find: a dot product from magnitudes and angle, or the angle from a dot product and two nonzero magnitudes.

7.4

Dot Product: Algebraic Vectors

For component vectors, multiply corresponding components and add. This formula works in both two and three dimensions. It also gives a useful test for perpendicular vectors and a way to calculate the angle between them.

(a₁, a₂, a₃) · (b₁, b₂, b₃) = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃

Example. Let a = (2, −1, 3) and b = (4, 2, 0). Then a · b = 2(4) + (−1)(2) + 3(0) = 6. Since the result is not zero, these nonzero vectors are not perpendicular.

Angle formula: cos θ = (a · b) / (|a||b|). Check that both vectors are nonzero before using it.

7.5

Scalar & Vector Projections

A projection measures the part of one vector that lies along another. The scalar projection is a signed length; the vector projection is a vector parallel to the direction being projected onto. A negative scalar projection means the component points opposite the chosen direction.

compb a = (a · b)/|b|     projb a = ((a · b)/|b|²)b

Example. Let a = (3, 4) and b = (1, 0), so b points along the positive x-axis. Then compb a = 3 and projb a = (3, 0).

Remember: The scalar projection has units of length (or the units of a); the vector projection has both magnitude and direction.

7.6

Cross Product of Two Vectors

For vectors in three dimensions, the cross product is a vector perpendicular to both inputs. Its direction follows the right-hand rule, and reversing the order reverses its direction. Parallel vectors have a zero cross product.

|a × b| = |a||b| sin θ

Example. Let a = (1, 2, 0) and b = (0, 1, 3). Then a × b = (6, −3, 1). Check the direction by confirming (a × b) · a = 0 and (a × b) · b = 0.

Magnitude meaning: |a × b| is the area of the parallelogram spanned by a and b.

7.7

Applications of Dot & Cross Products

The dot product measures how much a force acts along a displacement. The cross product models perpendicular area and the turning effect of a force. Together these connect vector calculations to physical and geometric quantities.

Work: W = F · d = |F||d|cos θ
Parallelogram area: A = |a × b|
Triangle area: A = ½|a × b|
Torque: τ = r × F, so |τ| = |r||F|sin θ

Example. A constant 10 N force acts in the direction of a 5 m displacement. The work is W = 10(5)cos 0° = 50 J. If the force were perpendicular to the displacement, the work would be zero.

Units: work is measured in joules (N·m); torque magnitude is measured in N·m and its vector indicates the rotation axis.
