Homework 01 · 7.1–7.2

Forces & velocity

1. Two forces of 9 N east and 12 N north act on a ring. Find the resultant's magnitude and direction, then give the equilibrant.

Show solution

R = (9, 12) N. Its magnitude is √(9² + 12²) = 15 N. The direction is tan⁻¹(12/9) ≈ 53.1° north of east. The equilibrant is (−9, −12) N, also 15 N, directed 53.1° south of west.

2. A kayak can move at 5 m/s relative to still water. A river flows east at 3 m/s. The kayak aims due north. Find its speed relative to shore and its downstream drift after 40 s.

Show solution

Ground velocity = (3, 5) m/s, so speed = √34 ≈ 5.83 m/s. Eastward drift in 40 s is 3(40) = 120 m.

3. An aircraft's air velocity is (0, 260) km/h and the wind velocity is (−40, 0) km/h, using east and north components. Find ground speed and bearing direction relative to north.

Show solution

Ground velocity = (−40, 260). Speed = √(40² + 260²) ≈ 263.1 km/h. The direction is tan⁻¹(40/260) ≈ 8.7° west of north.

Homework 02 · 7.3

Geometric dot product

1. Vectors u and v have magnitudes 8 and 5, and their included angle is 120°. Find u · v and interpret its sign.

Show solution

u · v = |u||v|cos120° = 8(5)(−1/2) = −20. The negative value indicates an obtuse included angle.

2. Nonzero vectors a and b satisfy |a| = 6, |b| = 4, and a · b = 12. Find the angle between them to the nearest degree.

Show solution

cos θ = (a · b)/(|a||b|) = 12/24 = 0.5, so θ = 60°.

3. A student claims that a · b = b · c proves a = c. Give a counterexample using nonzero vectors.

Show solution

In R², let a = (1,0), b = (0,1), and c = (2,0). Then a·b = 0 and b·c = 0, but a ≠ c. Equal dot products with one vector do not determine the other vector uniquely.

Homework 03 · 7.4–7.5

Algebraic dot product & projection

1. Let a = (2, −1, 3) and b = (1, 4, 0). Find a · b and classify the angle as acute, right, or obtuse.

Show solution

a·b = 2(1) + (−1)(4) + 3(0) = −2. Since the dot product is negative and neither vector is zero, the angle is obtuse.

2. Find the scalar and vector projections of p = (4, 3) onto q = (0, 2).

Show solution

p·q = 6 and |q| = 2. Scalar projection = (p·q)/|q| = 3. Vector projection = ((p·q)/|q|²)q = (6/4)(0,2) = (0,3).

3. Find a unit vector perpendicular to both u = (1, 1, 0) and v = (0, 1, 1).

Show solution

u×v = (1, −1, 1), with magnitude √3. A unit vector perpendicular to both is (1/√3)(1, −1, 1). Its negative is also valid.

Homework 04 · 7.6–7.7

Cross product & applications

1. Compute a×b for a = (2, 1, 0) and b = (1, −1, 3). Verify that the result is perpendicular to both vectors.

Show solution

a×b = (3, −6, −3). Check: (3,−6,−3)·(2,1,0)=6−6=0; and (3,−6,−3)·(1,−1,3)=3+6−9=0.

2. Find the area of the parallelogram and triangle spanned by u = (1, 2, 0) and v = (0, 1, 2).

Show solution

u×v = (4, −2, 1), so |u×v| = √21. The parallelogram area is √21 ≈ 4.58 square units; the triangle area is √21/2 ≈ 2.29 square units.

3. A 18 N force is applied at the end of a 0.35 m handle at 40° to the handle. Find the torque magnitude.

Show solution

|τ| = |r||F|sinθ = 0.35(18)sin40° ≈ 4.05 N·m.

4. A 75 N force moves a crate 12 m at an angle of 30° to the displacement. Find the work done by this force.

Show solution

W = |F||d|cosθ = 75(12)cos30° ≈ 779.4 J.
